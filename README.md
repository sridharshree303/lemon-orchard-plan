# Lemon Orchard Business Plan
## Nellore Region, Andhra Pradesh

## Market Background

Andhra Pradesh has strong commercial relevance for lemon cultivation, and the Nellore belt is a known trading region for citrus activity. Based on founder inputs, the Gudur and Podalakur markets in Nellore district together generate about Rs. 200 crore of turnover during the summer season, and lemon cultivation in Andhra Pradesh spans around 1.15 lakh acres. These local figures should be verified with state horticulture or market board data before using them in an investor deck.

The larger market opportunity is supported by broader public data:

- India is one of the world's largest producers of lemons and limes.
- Public FAOSTAT-linked summaries report global lemon and lime production at about 23.2 million tonnes in 2024, with India contributing about 3.8 million tonnes.
- Lemons have demand across multiple uses: fresh consumption, beverages, culinary use, pickles and preserved products, lemon oil, citric-acid-linked processing, and peel-based value-added products such as pectin.

This creates a strong long-term opportunity for both farm-level production and downstream branding or processing.

## The Opportunity

To build a scalable lemon orchard business in the Nellore region, starting with Rapur-side low-cost cultivable red-soil land, using high-density orchard planning, drip irrigation, automation, and phased scale-up.

The initial model is a 10-acre lemon orchard. The long-term model expands into aggregation, branding, direct sales, exports, and eventually lemon processing.

## Business Model Overview

The business is based on four advantages:

- High-density orchard planning can improve yield per acre and reduce unit cost over time.
- Bulk cultivation lowers input, irrigation, supervision, and procurement costs.
- Government support for horticulture, drip irrigation, and solar systems can reduce capital burden.
- A structured orchard can generate long-duration income, typically over 15 years or more after maturity.

The model is inspired by organized orchard methods used in advanced agricultural systems, especially around irrigation efficiency, canopy management, and structured plantation layout.

### Core Revenue Logic

- Revenue starts meaningfully from Year 5 onward.
- Yield increases with plant maturity.
- Summer harvest is the main earnings driver in this model.
- Branding, direct sales, and processing can later improve price realization beyond mandi sales.

## Implementation Plan

### Land & Region

The proposed land base is in the Rapur region of Nellore district, where low-cost cultivable red soil is available and horticulture expansion is practical.

### Government Support

The project may benefit from farmer support schemes such as:

- Drip irrigation subsidy
- Solar pump support
- Horticulture development incentives
- Kisan credit and farm finance support

Exact subsidy amounts and eligibility should be checked with the Andhra Pradesh horticulture department and local agriculture offices.

### Orchard Development Timeline

#### Years 0 to 4

This is the establishment and management phase.

Key work includes:

- Land preparation
- Fencing
- Bore or irrigation setup
- Drip irrigation installation
- Planting
- Training and pruning
- Nutrition and pest management
- Canopy development
- Labour supervision and protection

During this period, mixed farming can support cash flow, such as:

- Lemon + coconut
- Lemon + mirchi
- Lemon + grams
- Lemon + livestock integration where feasible

#### Year 5 onward

Commercial harvesting starts from around Year 5, with yield improving as the orchard matures.

### Proposed Varieties

Based on founder inputs, the following varieties are considered promising for yield and keeping quality:

- TAL-94-14
- CRS-21
- Petluru Pulusu
- Balaji

These should be finalized after consulting local horticulture experts, nursery availability, disease resistance, and market preference.

### Plantation Method

Founder assumption:
High-density plantation in rectangular method at 19 ft × 14 ft spacing.

Planning note:
At 19 ft × 14 ft spacing, each plant occupies 266 sq ft and one acre (43,560 sq ft) can theoretically accommodate about 163 plants.

Calculation:
43,560 / 266 ≈ 163 plants per acre

The planning assumption of 150 to 160 plants per acre is well within this layout and leaves room for pathways and field margins. For the financial model below, 150 plants per acre is used as a conservative baseline.

### Key Advantages

- Long-duration income potential from around Year 5 to Year 20 and beyond
- Yield generally improves as the trees mature
- Government schemes may reduce irrigation and energy cost
- Intercropping is possible in the pre-harvest years
- Creates local employment
- Can absorb agriculture graduates into field management and operations
- Drip irrigation and water automation reduce labour intensity
- Larger acreage lowers per-acre supervision and procurement cost

### Risks & Challenges

- Lemon harvesting requires skilled labour, especially in the summer peak season
- Labour shortage can affect timely picking
- The first 4 years need patient capital with limited orchard income
- Price fluctuations can affect annual returns
- Orchard performance depends on water, nutrition, pruning, and pest management quality
- Final plant density and variety selection must be technically validated

## Optimization & Scalability

The orchard model can scale from 10 acres to 100 acres or more over time.

### Farm-Level Optimization

- Standardized spacing and pruning
- Drip irrigation and fertigation
- Solar-based water systems
- Mechanized or semi-mechanized irrigation scheduling
- Better field supervision
- Labour planning during harvest season
- Crop-wise block planning by variety and maturity year

### Commercial Optimization

Selling only through mandi can reduce price realization due to local charges and commissions. Based on founder inputs, mandi-linked deductions can be around 12 percent. Direct branding, direct trade, and institutional buyers can improve net realization.

Possible commercial upgrades:

- Create a farm brand for premium lemons.
- Sort and grade produce before sale.
- Sell directly to wholesalers, retailers, and food businesses.
- Build long-term contracts with bulk buyers.
- Explore export channels to the Middle East for lemons and other agricultural raw products such as paddy, mirchi, green gram, black gram, and cereals.
- Supply directly to processing industries.

## Growth Roadmap

Once farm production is stable, the business can expand beyond cultivation into a broader lemon ecosystem.

### Phase 1: Orchard Asset Model

- Start with 10 acres
- Build proof of yield and unit economics
- Standardize cultivation model
- Establish local labour and management system

### Phase 2: Scale and Aggregation

- Expand acreage year on year
- Aggregate lemons from nearby farmers
- Create a grading and collection center
- Improve farmer pricing compared to mandi routes

### Phase 3: Processing and Market Hub

A lemon processing unit in the Rapur or Nellore belt can create a larger business opportunity.

Potential benefits:

- Attract nearby farmers to sell directly
- Reduce mandi dependency and related deductions
- Build a regional lemon trade hub
- Support products such as juice, peel-based products, oil extraction, and preserved lemon products
- Create a buyer-seller marketplace around the processing outlet

This can move the business from a farm-only model to a larger agriculture and supply-chain business with the potential to grow into a Rs. 10 crore to Rs. 50 crore opportunity over time.
